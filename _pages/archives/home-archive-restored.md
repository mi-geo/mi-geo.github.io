Hi, I am Teng Zhang, a spatial data scientist and quantitative geographer working on questions of inequality, legal access, mass violence, and urban environments.

I earned my PhD in Geography from [Michigan State University](https://geo.msu.edu/), where I worked with Prof. Siddharth Chandra and Prof. Ashton Shortridge. My work brings together spatial analysis, data science, and machine learning to study how inequality becomes visible across places, institutions, and networks.

Much of my current research focuses on uneven legal resources across southern U.S. states. That work draws on more than 200,000 records collected through [custom web crawlers I built](/portfolio/scraping/), and it feeds a broader project on legal access, attorney availability, and geographic inequality. You can explore the larger academic side of this work through [Academic](/academic/) or go directly to [Research](/research/).

My dissertation project, [*Unsafe Place: Spatial Analysis of Indonesian Genocide 1965-1966 in Java Timur*](/research/Indonesia/), used spatial quantitative methods to ask three basic but difficult questions about violence: where it happened, who carried it out, and how local structures shaped its geography.

Teaching is the other steady part of my academic life. I have taught geography, GIS, and data analysis in both online and in-person settings, and I care a lot about making technical ideas feel approachable without making them shallow. You can find that side of my work on the [Teaching](/teaching/) page.

Outside work, I am also a committed traveler. The [Travel](/travel/) section is where I keep a lighter record of places, routes, and visual notes, including a [travel globe](https://mi-geo.github.io/teng-globe/globe.html) that turns that record into something a little more interactive.
